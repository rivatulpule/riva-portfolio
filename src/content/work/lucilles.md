## The Challenge

Lucille's Barbecue faces strong initial customer acquisition driven by location, reputation, and occasion-based dining. But like many casual dining restaurants, the brand struggles with post-visit drop-off, customers don't return frequently enough to drive long-term value. The challenge was to identify *why* customers weren't returning, and to design strategies to increase repeat visits and long-term engagement.

## My Role & Approach

I worked directly with Lucille's VP of Marketing and her team to analyze customer behavior across the full post-purchase journey. My approach centered on primary research:

- Designed and fielded surveys reaching **more than 100 respondents across two datasets**.
- Analyzed dining behavior, visit frequency, and the specific decision drivers behind why guests chose to return, or didn't.
- Combined this with secondary research to ground the findings in broader hospitality and retention patterns.

## Research & Observations

A few distinct threads emerged from the data:

- Many customers visit Lucille's for specific occasions rather than as part of a regular habit.
- A lack of structured follow-up after a visit reduced the likelihood of a repeat visit.
- Menu variety created decision fatigue, particularly for new customers navigating the menu for the first time.
- Customers who had a strong first experience were significantly more likely to return, the first visit carried outsized weight in shaping long-term behavior.

## Key Insights

I defined the core problem as a **retention gap, not an acquisition issue.** Three behavioral barriers stood out:

- No habit formation after the first visit, nothing prompted a guest to think of Lucille's again.
- An overwhelming menu that led to indecision, especially for first-timers.
- Weak post-visit engagement, leaving no reason for a guest to return sooner rather than later.

The insight I kept coming back to: **people don't return because they forget, not because they didn't enjoy it.**

## Recommendations

I grounded my ideation in behavioral economics, reducing friction in decision-making, reinforcing positive first experiences, and creating cues for repeat behavior, and developed three strategic intervention areas:

**1. First-Time Customer Conversion**
Targeted incentives and offers for new visitors, paired with a more structured onboarding into the Lucille's experience.

**2. Habit Formation Through Events**
Sports-based promotions designed to create routine dining behavior, positioning Lucille's as a recurring social destination rather than a one-off choice.

**3. Menu Simplification & Choice Architecture**
Curated menu options to reduce overwhelm, guiding customers, especially first-timers, toward high-satisfaction items instead of an undifferentiated full menu.

<em>These are proposed strategies based on the research findings above. I have not implemented these interventions, and I don't have confirmed data on their impact, this section reflects recommendations only, not measured results.</em>

## Reflection

This project demonstrated how retention is driven not just by product quality, but by behavioral design. By reducing friction, reinforcing positive experiences, and introducing habit loops, brands can meaningfully increase customer lifetime value, but only if they treat retention as a specific, addressable problem rather than a byproduct of a good meal.

Working directly with Lucille's reinforced how behavioral insights can translate into practical, real-world business impact.
