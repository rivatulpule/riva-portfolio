## The Challenge

Jenan operates across highly competitive food categories where shoppers often make purchase decisions within seconds. The challenge was to understand how packaging, product communication, freshness cues, and in-store execution influence shopper perception — and to identify concrete opportunities for Jenan to strengthen differentiation at the point of purchase.

## My Role & Approach

As a Consumer Marketing Intern at Al Ghurair Foods, I analyzed NielsenIQ retail data across 15 UAE markets to identify category, pricing, market-share, and consumer trends informing brand and commercial strategy. My approach combined:

- **NielsenIQ analysis** across 15 UAE markets, synthesizing consumer, category, and competitor insights into executive-ready fact sheets for marketing and commercial teams.
- **In-market observational research**, evaluating packaging, shelf visibility, promotional execution, and shopper decision cues across Jenan and competitor categories.
- **Competitive benchmarking**, comparing Jenan's communication and retail execution against competitors across oats, flour, poultry, and adjacent categories.
- **Shopper behavior observation**, watching how people actually interacted with products at the shelf.

Throughout, one question guided the research: *what information helps a shopper understand why they should choose this product within seconds?*

## Research & Observations

I studied Jenan's products through both a shopper and brand lens, focusing on four areas: packaging communication and product differentiation, freshness communication and consumer perception, shelf visibility and in-store execution, and growth opportunities.

- **Oats**: Nutritional claims on Jenan Oats were present but visually understated compared to competitors.
- **Flour**: Distinguishing between flour variants required prior category knowledge — the range assumed a level of familiarity many shoppers didn't have.
- **Poultry**: Competitors made freshness cues significantly more prominent within their packaging than Jenan did.
- **In-store execution**: Promotional signage and secondary displays measurably increased visibility at shelf, and product knowledge from in-store promoters helped build shopper confidence.
- **A direct shopper moment**: production dates directly influenced purchase decisions in fresh poultry — I observed a shopper choose a competitor's chicken specifically because it displayed a newer production date, a clear example of how operational execution shapes brand perception at shelf.

## Key Insights

The research revealed that the opportunity wasn't simply greater brand visibility — the larger issue was reducing the amount of work shoppers had to do to understand the product. A few behavioral barriers stood out:

- Important benefits were easy to overlook, even when technically present on pack.
- Similar products within a range lacked obvious visual differentiation.
- Some product ranges assumed existing category knowledge shoppers didn't necessarily have.
- Freshness wasn't always communicated as strongly as competitors managed to.
- Shelf execution itself could create — or weaken — confidence at the exact moment of purchase.

The guiding principle behind my recommendations: **make the easiest information to notice the information most useful for making a decision.**

## Recommendations

**1. Benefit-Led Packaging (Jenan Oats)**
Increase the prominence of protein and fiber claims; introduce usage occasions such as overnight oats, breakfast bowls, and baking; create clearer differentiation between oat variants.

**2. Choice Architecture & Product Differentiation (Jenan Flour)**
Introduce application-based imagery and icons so shoppers can immediately identify which flour works for bread, pastries, cakes, and other uses; increase the visual prominence and contrast of individual variants.

**3. Freshness & Trust Cues (Jenan Chicken)**
Increase the prominence of freshness-related claims; explore larger transparent packaging windows to boost product visibility; strengthen local provenance messaging around Jenan's UAE farming. I developed a proposed packaging concept built around these recommendations — a large transparent product window at the center, with "100% UAE Farm Fresh Chicken" as one of the dominant visual messages.

**4. In-Store Experience**
Strengthen secondary branded visibility, promotional signage, product knowledge among promoters, and FIFO stock rotation to protect freshness perception.

## Growth Opportunities

Based on category trends, competitor offerings, promoter conversations, and observed shopper needs, I identified three potential whitespace opportunities:

- **Protein pasta** — aligned with growing interest in protein-focused foods and healthier meal alternatives.
- **Cooking oil spray** — a portion-controlled, convenient format already appearing among competitors.
- **Smaller chicken pack sizes** — in-store conversations suggested demand for smaller whole chickens, pointing toward 700–900g SKUs better suited to shopper and household needs.

<em>These are research-based recommendations I presented internally. They reflect proposed strategy, not confirmed business decisions or implemented changes.</em>

## Reflection

This project changed the way I think about the shelf. A purchase decision is influenced by much more than the product itself — packaging hierarchy, visual cues, product visibility, promotional execution, and even stock rotation can all change what a shopper notices, trusts, and ultimately chooses.

The biggest takeaway: good marketing reduces the amount of thinking a customer has to do. The most valuable recommendations weren't about communicating more — they were about identifying which information mattered most at the moment of choice, and making that information easier to see, understand, and act on.

This project reinforced why I enjoy consumer insights work: observing real behavior, finding the friction behind it, and translating those observations into changes a business can actually make.
